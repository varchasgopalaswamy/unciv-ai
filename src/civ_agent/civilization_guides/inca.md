## Turn difficult terrain into economic and tactical advantages

Terrace farms, available at Construction, add food on hills and gain another
food for each adjacent Mountain. Scout for workable mountain-adjacent hills,
enough usable surrounding land, and sites that can become productive without
excessive delay. Farms supply food while retaining the hill's base production;
compare them with mines and other improvements according to the city's needs.
Check tile eligibility and current yields instead of assuming every hill can
receive a Terrace farm or that mountains alone make a good city site.

Great Andean Road removes the extra terrain cost of entering hills, halves road
and railroad maintenance, and eliminates improvement maintenance on hill tiles.
Road construction still costs worker time. Favor useful connections and routes,
not unnecessary roads simply because hill upkeep is free. Hill mobility can
improve exploration, defense, reinforcement, and attacks through terrain that
slows opponents. Rivers, zones of control, visibility, and legal movement still
need inspection; the ability is not unrestricted movement.

Slingers replace Archers with the same ranged strength but lower melee strength,
3 rather than 4, and a chance to withdraw before melee combat. Withdrawal is
conditional and needs a legal destination; it is not a substitute for protective
units or retreat space. Use hills and ranged support to exploit positioning
without treating Slingers as a guaranteed early city-conquest unit.

An early campaign can be attractive when a reachable opponent and hilly
approaches create a real mobility advantage. Compare capturing valuable land
with claiming strong unoccupied sites. If there is no suitable early target,
productive hill cities and economical connections can finance a later army or
another victory path. The Incan advantage is persistent and terrain-dependent,
not a mandate either to rush immediately or to remain peaceful indefinitely.
