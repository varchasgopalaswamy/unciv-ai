## Use early culture to prepare later military or economic strength

Ancien Regime provides 2 culture per city until Steam Power. Earlier policies
can support expansion, production, military investment, or a cultural finish;
the bonus does not prescribe cultural victory. Additional cities still impose
happiness and policy-cost tradeoffs. Settle or capture productive locations
rather than expanding solely for the culture bonus. Plan for the bonus to end,
but do not postpone a valuable technology merely to preserve it.

Musketeers, available at Gunpowder, replace Musketmen with 20 strength rather
than 16 at the same base production cost. This can create an offensive window
against a reachable rival or let a smaller force defend efficiently. Prepare
ranged or siege support, reinforcement routes, and an attainable objective;
strong frontline units are not by themselves a complete siege plan.

In these Vanilla engine rules, the Foreign Legion replaces the Rifleman and
unlocks at Rifling, with 36 strength versus the Rifleman's 25 and a further 20%
strength bonus when fighting in foreign territory. It also has a higher base
production cost: 320 versus 225 before speed and other modifiers. This is a
substantial later offensive opportunity, but producing or upgrading a useful
force still takes resources. Verify current construction and upgrade details
rather than assuming another Civilization expansion's Foreign Legion rules.

Compare a prepared Gunpowder or Rifling campaign with peaceful alternatives
before the relative advantage disappears. Capturing a productive neighbor or
disrupting the likely winner may support any victory plan. Early culture can
help prepare that campaign rather than justify perpetual peace. If a cultural
finish is strongest instead, this ruleset uses completed policy branches and
the Utopia Project, not tourism or Chateaux.
