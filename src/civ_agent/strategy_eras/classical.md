# Classical era

## Strategy

Turn settlements into an empire that can support further growth. Compare the
next city with improving existing ones, national infrastructure and a useful
military opportunity. Choose a direction while keeping affordable alternatives
open; early scientific development benefits several victory routes.

## Economy

Improve worked tiles and repair happiness or income shortages before adding
costly commitments. Libraries and National College timing interact with expansion:
another city can postpone completion but later add output. Compare road income
and defensive value with upkeep and worker time. Give each worker useful tasks
and avoid infrastructure whose maintenance exceeds its present benefit.

## Military

Compare available melee, ranged and mobile forces with actual defenders and
terrain. An early advantage matters only if it can reach a useful target before
reinforcement. Protect ranged units, retain city occupiers and plan replacement
or withdrawal. Do not accumulate an army without a purpose or ignore its supply cost.

For example, a wounded city beside an assembled melee-and-ranged force can be a
short campaign using troops already paid for, while the capital keeps building.
The same apparent weakness across several turns of difficult terrain may vanish
before the army arrives. Compare arrival and capture time with healing and
reinforcement; name the preparation that would change that comparison.

## Diplomacy

Turn surplus resources and practical border understandings into useful exchanges.
Check exact terms, affordability and who gains more. Neighboring expansion can
justify bargaining, preparation or pressure without requiring automatic hostility.
Revisit an understanding after a material change, not through repeated confirmations.
