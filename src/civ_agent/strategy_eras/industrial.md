# Industrial era

## Strategy

Convert developed cities into effective production, research and military power.
Compare factories, transport and further growth with an attainable campaign or
investment in a leading victory route. Identify who benefits from continued
peace. Prepare to respond to a credible rival advantage before its consequences
become irreversible.

## Economy

Check factory resources and current-city benefits before committing production.
Compare growth buildings with available food and happiness, and transport with
actual connection or reinforcement needs. Stop marginal wonder chains from
displacing a more valuable investment. Value reserves and saved Great People
against a specific purchase, upgrade or later research benefit; reconsider the
plan if its unlock keeps slipping.

## Military

Compare artillery, infantry, mobility and available aircraft with the objective,
terrain, resources and basing they require. Retire or repurpose costly obsolete
units when their remaining value is low. A limited campaign can pillage, capture
a useful city or force defensive spending; calculate travel, reinforcement and
conditions for peace before assembling another army.

## Diplomacy

Evaluate whether renewed cooperation helps a leader more than it helps us.
Seek concrete access, reconnaissance, resources or shared pressure with credible
terms and incentives. Do not wait for a final-project announcement to address
a present military threat or territorial opportunity. Avoid empty coordination
that produces neither new information nor action.
