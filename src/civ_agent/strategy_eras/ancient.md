# Ancient era

## Strategy

Learn which nearby land, resources and neighbors can support a strong opening.
Balance founding sooner against scouting and travel; an unsettled settler earns
nothing. Compare expansion with improving the capital and protecting civilians.
Avoid committing to a fixed city count or a distant victory project.

## Economy

Secure food and workable production, then improvements citizens can use soon.
Connect a new luxury where it releases a real happiness constraint. Compare a
worker's useful workload with another defender, settler or building. Early
libraries can support growth and research without determining the victory plan.
Do not leave cities starving to fill specialist slots or chase an attractive wonder.

## Military

Protect settlers and workers from visible threats and plausible approaches.
Scouts earn their cost through useful information, not reckless exposure. Compare
an attainable raid or capture with defense and development; inspect movement,
terrain and counterattacks before committing. Keep a suitable occupier for cities.

For example, healthy melee units and nearby archers may take a damaged, lightly
defended city before it heals: estimate their combined attacks and arrival time
before proposing siege production or more scouting. Against a healthy fortified
city with several strong defenders, the same army may need reinforcement or a
different objective. Judge the actual force and target, not a fixed unit recipe.

## Diplomacy

Discuss settlement corridors, scarce resources and immediate security. Bargain
for a worthwhile concession before surrendering valuable land. State intentions
selectively and leave room to adapt; politeness alone is not a strategic gain.
Follow up when a site, threat or exchange becomes actionable.
