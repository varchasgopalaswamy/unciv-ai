**Gameplay reassessment, October 13, 2026**

Terra now shows more consistent infrastructure and happiness management, while
Luna retains the larger empires and produces several of the strongest individual
positions. Both models can execute useful tactical sequences. Neither has yet
demonstrated a decisive overall playing-strength advantage. Luna has a substantial
throughput advantage in the observed completed turns.

The fresh snapshots were captured at **13:41:48 UTC / 09:41:48 EDT**. Completed
rounds are 99, 93, 101 and 86 in `all-luna-attempt-2-0`, and 101, 104, 101 and 99
in `luna-terra-attempt-1-0`. The earlier October 13 snapshot covered rounds
36, 39, 39 and 32, and 76, 77, 70 and 73, respectively. These are ongoing games;
score leadership is not a win or a winning probability.

Each game has five model seats and one native control. The all-Luna batch has
20 Luna seats; the mixed batch has 12 Luna and eight Terra seats. Gameplay models
are `gpt-5.6-luna` and `gpt-5.6-terra`, with maximum reasoning effort. The games
use Prince difficulty, Standard speed, Vanilla, medium Pangaea and 12 city-states.
They share a terrain seed but have different placement/assignment seeds. Seat
averages are descriptive, and seats within a game are not independent trials.

all eight Terra seats have completed National Colleges, up from four in the
earlier snapshot. Luna has also caught up substantially, from one to eight of
12. Terra's current science advantage has consequently narrowed from about 25%
to 9%; its culture advantage has grown to about 51%.

Terra's lower technology count remains relevant: higher current science output
does not establish a cumulative research lead. Its smaller footprint is also
still visible. Nevertheless, maintaining nearly the same population and
production with fewer cities, while avoiding negative happiness throughout this
window, supports a favorable assessment of its economic management. Its average
score deficit to Luna has narrowed from about 30 to 20 points.

Luna's best positions should not disappear inside those averages. Mixed game 3
Persia added nine population and 16 production over 20 rounds. Mixed game 2
Arabia and all-Luna game 2 France are also developing strong economies. Conversely,
Terra's two Incan seats still have only two cities and low production. The game 3
seat has a Settler and a conditional route plan; game 4 Inca has actually founded
its second city and added eight population over the latest 20 rounds. The earlier
one-city criticism no longer describes that position.

**The all-Luna batch is competitive with its controls at this stage, with large
variation between seats.** Twelve of 20 model seats lead their native control's
score: none in game 1, and four in each remaining game. Mean population is 17.8
versus the controls' 17.25, production 33.4 versus 35.75, and science 34.3 versus
24.25. The research-output advantage is substantial, but technology count still
slightly trails, 14.65 versus 15.25. Nine of 20 National Colleges are complete.
This does not yet reproduce the severe population and production deficits seen
much later in the retired first-attempt batch; the different turn horizons also
prevent claiming that those later problems have been solved.

**The most persistent economic mistake is allowing bottlenecks to survive while
preserving reserves or pursuing another build.** All-Luna game 1 France's
turn-90 review identifies a National College with stored production that had
been displaced by a Worker, then blocked by another city's missing Library.
At the new cutoff, the College is still queued. Expansion without coordinating
the Library requirement has delayed the intended science payoff.

Mixed game 1 Inca has 811 gold, only one Worker, 20 production and an unfinished
National College; its turn-100 review estimates another 13 turns to finish it.
Arabia in the same game holds 807 gold and has endured 19 unhappy rounds out of
20, adding just one population. It has recently recovered to positive happiness,
but its Library and College sequence remains late. All-Luna game 4 China's
1,227-gold reserve also deserves a concrete comparison against purchases and
expansion. A large treasury alone is not a mistake; these cases combine it with
observable development constraints. The evidence does not establish the optimal
purchase without evaluating available prices, alternatives and reserve needs.

Happiness recovery is uneven. All-Luna game 3 China was unhappy in 17 of the
latest 20 rounds and added only one population; it is now positive. The mixed
batch's Terra seats have no negative-happiness rounds in that window. These
differences matter more than whether a review correctly names happiness as a
priority: the outcome is how quickly growth resumes.

**Combat now supplies evidence beyond intentions.** All-Luna game 3 Arabia
declared on native France at turn 91. Its receipts confirm six French unit kills
through turn 98: two Archers, two Scouts, a Spearman and a Swordsman. It repeatedly
uses city bombardment and ranged fire before committing melee units. Its own
Archer disappears from the owned roster after turn 94, so this is not evidence
of a lossless campaign. At turn 101 a Pikeman counterattack leaves a French
Horseman at 10 health; that is damage, not a seventh confirmed kill.

All-Luna game 3 Greece declared on Inca at 99 and destroyed Slingers at 100 and
101 through coordinated city/unit attacks. Mixed game 3 Terra Greece captured
an Arabian Worker at 99 with a Scout. The immediate attack caused no damage,
but Damascus then dealt 74 damage to the Scout and an Arabian Archer destroyed
it. Greece still owns the captured Worker and moved it away on turns 100 and
101. This is a Worker-for-Scout exchange with an ongoing war, not a free raid.
It exposes a concrete planning weakness: a favorable immediate combat preview
does not account for the opponent's next turn. None of the model seats currently
holds a foreign-founded city, so these actions demonstrate raids and border
combat rather than successful retained city conquest.

**The feedback mechanisms are visible, but the outcomes remain inconsistent.**
Recent reviews distinguish queued construction from completion and accepted
attacks from confirmed kills. For example, mixed game 1 Inca's review explicitly
notes that a 3-HP Brute remains alive after the accepted attack. Historical
progress includes changes in happiness, exploration, construction and units.
That supplies useful evidence to the player; the remaining delayed Colleges and
idle reserves show that supplying evidence does not guarantee timely decisions.

Exploration also needs a more selective assessment. All-Luna game 4 China
revealed 185 tiles over its latest 20 rounds, a substantial recovery from its
earlier problem. Across the batch, however, eight of 20 Luna seats revealed fewer
than ten tiles. Mixed-batch counts are three of 12 Luna and one of eight Terra.
Some low exploration is explained by war or geography, so these counts identify
positions to examine rather than proving every Scout was mismanaged.

No captured model city is running Gold, Science or Culture conversion. This is
consistent with the enforced final-era restriction. It is not independent
evidence that the models would now choose infrastructure voluntarily, and these
runs do not provide a controlled estimate of the five fixes' effects.

**Diplomacy still devotes substantial attention to distant victory projects.**
Across the latest 20-round windows, 114 of 225 unique all-Luna messages (51%) and
73 of 223 mixed messages (33%) mention Apollo, spaceship, Utopia, terminal or
United Nations/UN. The keyword count includes useful mixed-topic messages; it
does not label every matching message useless. Repeated reports of projects
that have not begun nevertheless remain prominent at roughly turn 100.

Real trades occur: the captured current-timeline shared proposals contain 17
enacted versus 35 expired in all-Luna, and 12 enacted versus nine expired in
mixed. These are proposal-version outcomes, not a conversion rate for every
offer ever made. Expiration is a host deadline outcome; these records alone do
not separate scheduling/delivery delays from player inaction. Conversation
context should be judged by completed negotiations, not message volume alone.

**Terra's gains come with substantially slower observed turns.** In the mixed
batch's latest 20 completed rounds, median gameplay time is 238 seconds for
Terra versus 65 seconds for Luna, about 3.6 times as long. Mean reported input
plus output is about 620,000 versus 315,000 tokens, including cached input;
uncached input is about 80,000 versus 55,000. This is not a monetary cost ratio.
The sample contains 160 Terra and 240 Luna completed gameplay jobs and excludes
chat, reviews, incomplete retries and finalization. The development results do
not yet establish an overall strength advantage sufficient to offset that
throughput difference; a completed-game comparison remains necessary.

At the **13:55 UTC operational recheck**, all four all-Luna games and mixed games
2–4 were running. Mixed game 1 remained paused after five automatic retries for
unavailable model capacity. Mixed game 3 had recovered from the capacity retry
observed at capture. These are operational conditions, not gameplay defeats.

The next gameplay checkpoints should examine whether the remaining Colleges and
Universities actually finish, whether newly positive happiness produces growth,
whether large reserves address specific constraints, and whether wars yield
retained benefits after casualties and disruption. Raw score and immediate
attack success are insufficient by themselves.

**Evidence and limits.** The audit copied atomic turn-recovery saves and used
read-only SQLite transactions. Comparisons use each game's last fully completed
round; city assets and queues come from the associated boundary save. Science
and culture use the stored completed-turn outputs. Production includes current
construction modifiers and temporary effects, so it is not normalized capacity.
Command receipts, reviews and reachable observation histories are limited to
the cutoff. Telemetry deduplicates completed turns across recovery attempts;
diplomacy counts use the captured current recovery epoch.

The native save omits default `diplomaticStatus: War` values. This extraction
correctly restores that default; omitted status must not be interpreted as
peace. The earlier snapshot's derived war lists should not be used for historical
peace/war comparisons. No runtime, gameplay configuration or game state was
changed during this assessment.
