The earlier population advantage was a specific city-management tradeoff. In the first Inca position, Machu grew from 9 to 10 for both first-repetition baselines. Luna’s candidate enabled avoid growth; Terra’s candidate selected ProductionFocus. Both reduced food surplus from about 6 to 1 per turn and finished Machu’s University earlier. Their final native science rates were 82 versus 75 (Luna) and 78 versus 75 (Terra). The growth and happiness data were present in the candidate context. Luna later saw empire happiness +5 and retained avoid growth; the records establish no reversal, not whether it silently reconsidered. The second repetition repeated Luna’s one-citizen gap; Terra had no growth in either variant. Detailed dated evidence is in paired-retry-1/growth-analysis.json and growth-analysis.txt.

Across all 16 matched pairs, candidate retained-city growth was lower in four, equal in eleven, and higher in one. Terra’s final Arabia candidate gained one more citizen than its baseline. This is a recurring tradeoff in particular positions, not a universal growth outcome.

Luna’s first Arabia baseline added a Public School, Lighthouse and Great Lighthouse in Medina that its candidate did not, while its candidate retained 950 more gold. Their second Arabia repetition had equal building counts, and the candidate acquired one extra technology. Treasury choices and the value of individual technologies/buildings limit comparisons based on counts alone.

Terra gained two cities in the later Inca position in every variant/repetition. Its first-repetition candidate lost two military units versus one for baseline; the second repetition lost one each. Luna’s first candidate lost an exposed Pikeman that its baseline withdrew. The candidate’s startup explicitly contained that unit’s health, nearby enemies, movement availability and Military ownership. In China, Luna’s first baseline declared war and upgraded units, lost a Warrior and gained no city; its candidate stayed at peace. Different action volume and strategic choices contribute to token differences.
